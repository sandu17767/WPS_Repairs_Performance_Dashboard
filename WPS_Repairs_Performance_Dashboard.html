About this dashboard. Built from a practice dataset I designed and analysed end-to-end — 8 linked tables, 21,001 repair jobs, 2,514 homes, 5 client contracts, 12 months (Jul 2025 – Jun 2026) — modelled on how a social-housing repairs contractor is measured: on-time performance, the Regulator of Social Housing's Tenant Satisfaction Measures, and Awaab's Law statutory deadlines. Every figure reconciled three ways (BigQuery SQL · Excel Power Query · Power Pivot) to the row and to the penny.

The finding

Eight trades hold 89–92%. Damp & mould sits ~30 points below.

The healthy trades carry most of the volume, so the weakness never shows in the headline. Damp & mould is also the one trade where a missed date is a statutory breach, not just a KPI miss.

When it started

The break point is 27 October 2025 — Awaab's Law Phase 1

Demand tripled (2.5 → 8.9 damp jobs/day) and the legal completion window roughly halved (26.5 → 13.4 days) against the same four damp specialists. The rolling three-month rate has been flat between ~54% and ~59% for six months: it has stopped falling — and it is not recovering on its own.

Root cause

Six alternative explanations — five ruled out

Before naming a cause, each plausible explanation was tested against the data. The team did not get worse — the bar moved faster than a four-person specialist team could follow.

General decline across the business?
Ruled out
The other eight trades held 89–92% every single month.
One bad client contract?
Ruled out
All five contracts sit within 1.1 points overall (86.3% – 87.4%).
Subcontractor quality?
Ruled out
Direct staff 61.2% vs subcontractors 60.9% — no gap.
Did the team slow down?
Ruled out
The opposite — average days per damp job fell 15.0 → 13.2.
Did the target change?
Contributing cause
Awaab's Law roughly halved the window: 26.5 → 13.4 days.
Did demand rise?
Contributing cause
Damp jobs went 2.5 → 8.9 per day (×3.6) — same four specialists.

Recommendations

Five actions — each with a measure that proves it worked

The obvious answer — hire more people — was modelled and rejected: the arithmetic supports a cheaper fix first.

1

Give statutory Awaab's Law cases their own prioritised queue

The jobs with the highest legal consequence are currently handled worse than average (56.4% vs 86.9%).

PROVE IT ✓ statutory on-time rate climbing toward the headline; breaches falling month on month
2

Fix damp scheduling as a business-wide process

All five contracts show the same weakness — this is a process problem, not an account problem.

PROVE IT ✓ rolling 3-month damp rate climbing for three consecutive months
3

Brief Riverbourne before the June 2027 renewal

Their damp on-time rate is the lowest (53.0%) on the second-largest contract.

PROVE IT ✓ the account review opens with our numbers and our plan — not their complaint
4

Report the rolling 3-month rate, not the monthly one

Monthly damp rates swing up to 26 points — a bounce is too easily read as a trend (I made that mistake once, and caught it).

PROVE IT ✓ one agreed trend number everyone reads the same way
5

Publish satisfaction split by on-time vs late, monthly

The 36.6-point recommend gap is the tenant-facing cost of lateness — track it where leadership sees it.

PROVE IT ✓ the on-time vs late recommend gap narrowing
✕

Tested and rejected: "hire more damp operatives"

A capacity model (Goal Seek) showed the four specialists already meet the throughput — 13.2 days actual vs 13.4-day target on jobs they reach. It is a scheduling and backlog problem, not headcount.

WHY IT MATTERS ✓ the cheaper answer — and the one the arithmetic supports
